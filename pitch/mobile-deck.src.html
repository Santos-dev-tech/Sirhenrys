Why it matters

Both of your audiences are holding a phone

Your customers

A man deciding whether to spend eighty thousand shillings on a suit is usually doing it one-handed, on a phone, somewhere other than a desk.

Your shop floor

Your staff are standing next to the customer with a tape measure, not sitting behind a counter. What they carry is a phone.

Everything in this deck is one of those two people trying to do something ordinary, and what happened when they did.

Where this started

We flagged this ourselves

“The console is desktop-shaped. Shop floor staff carry phones. The till works on one; the tables do not.” Our own audit of the build

We wrote that, and we were too kind to ourselves. When we went back and checked properly, the till did not work on a phone at all. Nor did the shop front.

What follows is that correction, and the repair.

What it was costing

Nobody was going to tell you

A customer who cannot tap your menu does not email you about it. He does not ring the shop. He leaves, and you never find out he was there.

  • —
    Every customer arriving on a phone met a page they had to pinch and zoom to read a price on.
  • —
    Every one who opened the menu got a block of running text where the links should have been.
  • —
    Every fitting on the shop floor had to be carried back to a desktop to be rung up.

This was live for weeks and produced no complaints, no support calls and no bad reviews — because a broken phone produces silence, not feedback. The only way it was ever going to be found was somebody going looking for it.

Those checks now run automatically on every change. The same fault cannot come back without stopping the work that caused it.

56 security and interface checks, and colour contrast in light and dark, across every page — every time.

A shop's website is not a delivery you take once. It is a thing that has to keep working on phones that did not exist when it was built.

Being straight with you

What this does not fix

Four things a phone still deserves, none of them touched by this work.

  • 1
    Image weight. A phone on a Nairobi mobile connection still downloads the same full-size photographs a desktop does. This is the single biggest remaining cost to a shopper on data, and it is mechanical work rather than a redesign.
  • 2
    The till still needs a connection. A till that stops when the signal drops is a till that stops the queue. The groundwork is there; finishing it is a separate piece.
  • 3
    M-Pesa is still simulated. Unchanged by this work, and still the case as pitched.
  • 4
    Staff sign-in is still demo-grade. The console says so on screen, on every page, until it is switched to real staff accounts.

Anyone who tells you a system is finished has not built one.
